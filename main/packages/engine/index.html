engine

engine

The pure-Go (CGO_ENABLED=0) TeX engine. A faithful re-implementation of TeX that loads and runs the genuine LaTeX classes — \documentclass{article}, {report} and {book} execute the real, embedded .cls files (LPPL, verbatim) — not an emulation — in native builds and in the browser via js/wasm, with no TeXLive and no server.

What it does

  • Runs the standard base classes for real. article, report and book load and execute the genuine embedded LaTeX .cls files, typesetting a numbered title, a \tableofcontents shaped by the class’s own \l@… macros (the indent and number-box widths are read out of its \@dottedtocline calls, so article’s leaderless bold \l@section and book’s dotted one at the same level come out differently), numbered sections/subsections, \chapter (report/book, with its entry in the contents list and its opening page’s folio at the foot), numbered figure/table captions, itemize/enumerate, and math.
  • A full pipeline. A category-code mouth and gullet, a scaled-point stomach with Knuth–Plass line breaking and a cost-based page builder, \halign tables, math via math (vector output), OpenType fonts (a built-in default font, kerning, ligatures), and PDF + SVG output — the SVG carries a source map for click-to-line.
  • Floats and two columns are the default. Real float placement (top, bottom and float-page, with the seven parameters read from the class rather than from article’s) and the two-column layouts that classes set themselves (revtex reprint, acmart sigconf/sigplan, IEEEtran) are on without a flag; GOTEX_FLOATS=0 and GOTEX_TWOCOLUMN=0 opt back out. This page described both as still behind their environment variables, which they have not been for some time.
  • Spacing and breaking commands behave as LaTeX’s own. \hspace and \vspace take a glue, not a rigid length, so \hspace{\fill}, \vspace{\stretch{2}} and \hspace{1cm plus 2mm} keep their stretch; \fill, \stretch, \enskip and \samepage are defined, with \fill and \stretch at the reference’s second order of infinity. \pagebreak, \nopagebreak, \linebreak and \nolinebreak contribute the penalties LaTeX gives them, the optional degree [0…4] included. \marginpar reads both of its arguments instead of letting them reach the page as text. (v0.263.1)
  • \\ carries its star and its optional length, and \vadjust works. \\[20pt] used to typeset the five characters [20pt] and \\* a *; both are now scanned, and the length produces the reference’s 31.88pt baseline step where a bare \\ gives 11.96. \vadjust{…} puts vertical material written inside a paragraph into the enclosing vertical list after the line it was written on — measured against tectonic as a 41.84pt step against a normal 11.96 — and it is an error in vertical mode, as TeX requires. Two commands came with it: \pagebreak and \nopagebreak now work when written on a line and not only between paragraphs (the reference makes two pages on a short first paragraph, and so do we, where we made one), and \\* refuses a page break after its line.
  • In the browser. A self-contained js/wasm build renders real LaTeX classes client-side — no TeXLive, no server.

Pipeline stages

Each stage is gated by an objective oracle.

StageStatusWhat it covers
Mouth + gullet✅ doneCategory-code tokenizer, eqtb, macros (delimited parameters, full expansion), conditionals, registers, grouping.
Stomach✅ doneBox/glue/penalty model in scaled points, Knuth–Plass line breaking, a cost-based page builder that keeps TeX’s two page measurements apart — page_total, which the break is decided on, and page_depth, which is not charged against the goal and is capped at \maxdepth — and \halign tables.
Math✅ done$…$ and display environments delegated to math (vector output).
Fonts✅ doneOpenType via go-opentype; a built-in default font, kerning and ligatures — runs with no assets.
Output✅ donePDF (embedded subset fonts, selectable text) and self-contained SVG pages with a click-to-line source map.
Real classes✅ done\documentclass{article|report|book} loads and runs the genuine embedded LaTeX class, native and js/wasm.

Fidelity gates

  • a byte-exact conformance ratchet (TestConformance), TeX snippets checked byte-for-byte against real-TeX output; and

  • a whole-document prose fidelity check against a real LaTeX engine (tectonic). On a corpus of 154 real arXiv papers with a reference PDF each, a vocabulary-bounded word census puts the median at 101% of the reference engine’s prose words (measured 2026-10-04); 10 of the 154 fall below 95% and 3 below 90%. Pagination lands on the reference exactly for 32 of them, with a total page deviation of 304 over the corpus (v0.261.0, font-controlled).

    In the default configuration — a different measurement, not an update of the one above, and the two must not be mixed — v0.263.0 lands exactly on 36 of the 154 with Σ|deviation| 294 and a signed bias of +72 pages. The bias is where the work is: it is a one-column phenomenon (#622), and v0.263.0 took 7 pages off it by keeping page_total and page_depth apart in the page builder. A page whose material reached exactly \textheight had been refused over the descender of its own last line, and lost a line (tex.web 19519-19529, 19594-19599).

    A median above 100% is expected rather than flattering: the engine emits each formula’s source as an invisible text layer so that maths can be found, copied and read aloud, and the census counts it. The figure to watch is the tail — the papers below 95% — not the median.

    That text layer is also why a PDF text extractor cannot be used to check mathematics against another engine: on a formula it returns our source, not glyph positions, so it compares two different things. Use the vector output, or the engine’s own box metrics, when the question is how a formula is set.

    v0.263.1 is not measured in these terms yet. It carries the spacing and breaking work described above, and the one piece of it measured on its own — \\ no longer ejecting a page in vertical mode — moved Σ|deviation| from 294 to 283, the bias from +72 to +59, and the exact papers from 36 to 39. That figure belongs to that change against its own control, not to the release: a sum assembled from separate deltas drifts, so the release’s own number is left blank until the release itself is swept.

    v0.265.0 was swept whole, which is the only way one of these numbers earns its place: 154 documents in the default configuration, Σ|deviation| 276, signed bias +50, 38 exact. It is not comparable to the 294/+72/36 above, which was measured at v0.263.0 on the same corpus but before the footnote work; the figures that are comparable are its own control — the commit immediately before the last of the four, which already carried the other three — at 277/+49/38. That control is not v0.264.0, and saying so matters: v0.264.0 predates all four changes, so a reader who took it for the baseline would credit this release with work three earlier merges had already done.

  • a geometry check, which is the only one of the three that can see text drawn outside the sheet. A block that should fill a title page used to be set as one horizontal list that was never line-broken, so everything past the right margin was placed off the paper — up to x=6619pt on a 612pt page, author names, affiliations and e-mails among it. The page count cannot see that (the clearest witness is 15 pages against 15), the ink count cannot (every glyph is emitted) and the text layer carries the words. Measured as lines ending beyond any page width this corpus uses: 49 papers at v0.260.0, 23 at v0.261.0, 22 at v0.262.0, against a floor of 2 on the reference PDFs themselves. The widest line went from 6619pt at v0.260.0 to 3357pt at v0.261.0 and 1788pt at v0.262.0.

  • a render check: a sha256 over the bytes of every page’s SVG, so it covers every coordinate on the page. Every sweep had recorded one since the beginning and nothing had ever compared two, so the page-count comparisons were the only ones being read — and they are blind by construction to anything that moves material within a page. That blindness reports such a change as “no document moved”, which reads as the change does nothing when the truth is the instrument cannot see it. Five changes measured since: 2, 25, 72, 40 and 72 of the 154 corpus documents render differently, and across all five exactly one document moved by a page (engine#675). That one is worth the sentence it takes: 2209.13121 went 36 pages to 37 against a reference of 42, so Σ|deviation| fell 277 → 276 while the signed bias rose +49 → +50 — the bias rose because the paper was short, not because anything got worse. It answers did the output change, not is it better: the reference’s PDFs are not our SVGs, so there is no third hash to be right about.

Roadmap

The engine loads and runs the standard base classes; it is functional-parity-oriented and held to real-LaTeX fidelity, not a claim of full TeXLive parity.

  • Heavy packages (tikz, …) run as real files rather than native stubs; TikZ/pgf drawing is gated behind GOTEX_PGF and in bring-up.

  • Front matter that still reaches past the page edge: 22 of the 154 corpus papers, down from 49, with five causes closed and the remainder a different population each (engine#629). The fifth cause is \unvbox inside a paragraph: a vbox holds finished lines, and handing them to the line breaker glues them end to end and re-breaks the lot as one paragraph (acmart writes \noindent\unvbox\mktitle@bx).

  • How far from the kernel, measured. latex.ltx — the assembled LaTeX kernel tectonic itself executes — defines 1952 control sequences. This engine and go-tex/math carry 755 of them; 1197 are absent, of which 642 are written by at least one paper in the 21 956-paper corpus. That is the shape of “not full TeXLive parity”, in numbers rather than in a disclaimer (engine#677). The list is a reading order, not a defect list. An earlier reading of the same inventory put it at 1256 absent and 695 used: the difference is three asymmetries in the reader, each of which made the engine look poorer than it is, and each found by taking three names off the head and checking them by hand.

    Ranking the absences by corpus demand turned out to rank noise. The first fourteen entries are names this engine already writes down somewhere — a special case in Go (\multicolumn, 51 of the 154 measured papers, is supported without being defined), a test file of its own (\baselinestretch, 41), or a comment saying why the name is deliberately absent (\@evenfoot and \@evenhead, 42 and 41: this engine has no two-sided page model). \do (46) is a loop hook every caller \lets to its own action. The measurement tool now marks such a name cited — absent as a definition, but already thought about — and 221 of the 642 used absences are cited. Nothing is filtered: the citation is a column beside the demand, because dropping a cited name would be a false presence, and a false presence deletes work for good where a false absence costs one reading.

    The list has no safe head, and that is the useful thing it says. The first absence this engine writes down nowhere at all is \@citea/\@citeb, 18 of the 154 — and defining them would accomplish nothing: they are the loop variables of \@citex, which this engine never executes, \cite being written in Go. A name internal to a macro replaced wholesale is invisible to both tests, and only reading the caller finds it. The defect in that neighbourhood is a real one of a familiar shape — 2941 papers, 22 of the 154, redefine the kernel’s citation internals and are ignored, exactly as \footnoterule used to be (engine#681) — but it is not the one the ranking named. Every entry on this list needs its caller read before it is believed.

  • A footnote is laid out by its class. The mark is whatever \thefootnote expands to, so \renewcommand\thefootnote{\fnsymbol{footnote}} works and a title block’s \thanks comes out ∗; and the note itself is now built by calling the class’s \@makefntext instead of placing the mark in Go, so the 1.8em box that right-aligns it (article.cls:609-612) is there. Before, every note’s text began one box-width to the left of the reference’s: no box of 14.400pt existed anywhere on the page, and one does now. What remains is the mark’s size: \@textsuperscript is the identity here, so it is set on the baseline at text size where the reference raises it, and \@fnsymbol gives an ASCII * where the reference sets * in math mode and draws U+2217.

  • 6.1pt of leftover on a page that stretches. With vertical infinite glue now contributing and every page packed exactly to \vsize, \vspace{\fill} puts its word at y=658.6 against tectonic’s 664.7 — the remaining third of (engine#663), which described three defects under one title.

  • A \marginpar’s note is read but not placed in the margin. Its arguments no longer reach the page as text and the body matches the reference exactly; putting the note itself out in the margin goes through LaTeX’s float machinery and is still to come.

  • A broader real-document conformance corpus (PDF-diff against pdftex/xetex) and the TRIP test.

Install 

go get github.com/go-tex/engine

Requires Go 1.26.4 or newer. CGO_ENABLED=0, go vet clean, CI green across three 64-bit arches under QEMU (riscv64/ppc64le/s390x) plus macOS, Linux and Windows, and both js/wasm and wasip1/wasm.
